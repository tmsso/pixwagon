Part A · authoritative

Corrective surfaces — the real mechanic

Every round the referee issues two offers, both revealed up front: the pair (two polyominoes, placed together or not at all) and the single (one custom-die value — one blob, or two independent blobs for a compound face). The player picks one, freely, every round. Surfaces keep their numbers; 07 is new. Built against shapes.ts, placement.ts, RollControl.tsx, PlacementEditor.tsx and the real Transportation pack.

Annotation 01 · unchanged

The board is the hero; the choice sits in thumb reach

Every screen puts its primary action in the lower half of 390×844. All targets ≥ minTouchTarget (2.75rem). Context and state live in the upper half, where a thumb does not reach.

Annotation 10 · new

“The pair” and “the single”, not “fallback”

RollControl’s label="Fallback" frames the die as error recovery. It is not: the player chooses between the two offers every round, deliberately.

Ship The pair / The single — plain, parallel, and each describes what you get rather than when you’d resort to it. OfferChoice’s wire values ('pair' | 'fallback') stay as they are; this is display copy only.

Copy to retire everywhere: “roll a combo”, “dice”, “fallback”, “if the pair doesn’t fit”.

Annotation 09 · new

The on-board gesture, finally specified

Phase 2 inferred this from BoardCell.candidate. It is now design, not inference:

Pair. Tap any board cell to drop the active piece’s origin there. Tapping elsewhere moves it — never adds a second copy. Turn and Flip act on the placed ghost in place, pivoting around the origin cell.

Single. Taps toggle individual cells into the active blob; tapping a chosen cell removes it. Contiguity is checked at commit, not per tap, so a player can backtrack through an invalid detour (placement.ts already behaves this way).

Placing the active piece hands focus to whichever piece is still unplaced — the two-tap path needs no explicit “switch piece” step.

Annotation 12 · new

The footer stack resolves as a sheet

Board + offers + editor + pass + rejection cannot coexist at full size on 390×844. So: the offers stay in the footer, and PlacementEditor rises over the board as a sheet with a scrim — a fixed 284px (34% of 844), the same height for pair and single composition so switching offers never resizes it.

The board never changes size across 03a–03f — 269×185px at every step — and it is never occluded. It does move once: opening the sheet re-centres it 76px upward into the space above the sheet, one settle at the start of composing, and then nothing moves while the player is placing. Resizing the board mid-placement, or letting the gap above the sheet change between offers, is what this rules out. The board area reserves exactly the sheet height (284px) so that gap is identical in every composition state.

Sheet transition uses duration.slow; under prefers-reduced-motion the tokens zero and the sheet simply appears.

Annotation 04 · unchanged

Disabled, never hidden

03f keeps every control mounted at full height and drops to 55% opacity. Hiding the sheet mid-flight would collapse it and drag the board down at exactly the wrong moment.

Annotation 13 · new

Both offers, always, in that order

The pair sits left, the single right, in every state — including states 1, 2 and 8 where neither is actionable. Nothing is ever gated behind declining: in same-board mode a decline-to-reveal would leak the single’s value to slower players purely by reaction speed.

“5 squares” / “1 + 2 squares” under each glyph is the one number a player needs and the reason pendingCellCount exists. State 8 keeps both offers on screen, dimmed, and adds Pass — it does not empty the footer.

Annotation 11 · new

Board scaling: three integer steps

cellSize is not fluid. It steps 20 → 28 → 40 at 768px and 1180px, and nothing in between — a Canvas board with a fractional cell size renders soft edges, which is the one thing a pixel-placement game cannot afford.

Pick the step, then centre the board in its area; never scale to fill. When the picture’s grid does not divide the viewport evenly the leftover space is margin, not a wider cell. If a picture at the chosen step would exceed the available width, drop one step rather than fitting to width.

Cells keep a 1px gap and no corner rounding, no per-cell shadow. imageSmoothingEnabled = false, and snap the whole board to whole device pixels at devicePixelRatio.

Annotation 14 · new

Composition copy, not debug output

Retire placed at (3, 5). A player who just tapped a square knows where it is; the coordinate was only ever useful to whoever wrote the reducer. Its slot now reads state: Tap the board → On the board.

Retire 2/3 and blob 1. Blob progress reads 1 more square while short and 2 of 2 · done when full, with a dot row showing the count. What a player needs is how many are left, not a fraction.

Take back clears the active piece or blob only — that is clearActive, and it is the undo-before-commit path. Cancel abandons the whole choice and returns to the offers.

Annotation 15 · new

All or nothing, said plainly

A finished picture scores its fixed value; an unfinished one scores zero. There is no partial credit, so results copy must never imply progress was worth something — 05b shows a 0 next to an 88%-filled board on purpose.

And it must not read as failure. A board that strands cells is a property of the mechanic, not a mistake, so the line names the cause (“nothing fitted those last three”) and offers the same picture again.

Sessions end on a precomputed round budget across the pack’s pictures — 05c is the real end screen; 05a is one picture inside it.

Annotation 16 · new

Offline is a state, not an incident

Nothing turns red, nothing blocks, nothing modal. 09b is byte-for-byte the ordinary solo game — a solo board has no server to miss, so showing a connection indicator there would be inventing a problem.

Home gains one neutral Offline pill and one line telling the truth about what is unavailable: playing with friends. Never “no internet connection” as a title.

The install prompt appears after a completed picture — the one moment the app has earned it — never on first load. “Not now” suppresses it for 30 days; a second dismissal retires it permanently and leaves only the quiet ghost button in 08b. No account exists to remember, so this lives in local storage and that is fine.

Annotation 17 · new

First paint, and motion that can be switched off

The splash is the cached shell: mark, wordmark, and a determinate bar only if boot exceeds 400ms — below that it flashes and is worse than nothing. Background is bg, so the splash and Home share a background and the handoff is invisible.

The update bar is a banner, not a dialog: a service-worker update must never interrupt a round. If a game is in progress it waits for the results screen.

Every animation here reads duration.*. Under prefers-reduced-motion those tokens zero and each has a static equivalent — the boot bar becomes a static label, the sheet appears without sliding, the rolling shimmer becomes a plain “Rolling…”.

Annotation 18 · new · provisional

Six seats, and why the number is six

The cap is playerColors.length — six hues that stay distinguishable under the common colour-vision deficiencies, each with its own hatch. The repo currently has two definitions of the maximum (MAX_PLAYERS in tokens.ts and a MAX_SEATS elsewhere); ROADMAP Phase 4 flags it. Six, derived from the colour list, one definition.

02e says so in player words rather than exposing a limit: “six is the most we can tell apart on a board.”

Annotation 02 · unchanged, extended

Hatch travels with hue, everywhere

Every seat swatch in Parts A, B and C uses patternStyle() geometry — solid, diagonal, dots, horizontal, cross, vertical — not just a hex fill. The board Canvas reproduces the same geometry.

Extended this pass to cover forward-designed surfaces: lobby chips, presence rows, own-board comparison tiles and rank rows all carry the pattern.

Annotation 19 · new · provisional

Presence, turn, and a rollback nobody else sees

Whose turn it is rides on the player chip — an accent ring plus the board hint naming them (“Sam is placing”). Turn is never colour alone, and never only in a corner of the header.

An optimistic fill is local until the referee answers: other clients receive the move only after it is accepted, so a rejected fill never appears on anyone else’s board. That is why the ghost state exists as a distinct cell state from filled — the ghost is the un-broadcast one.

A player who drops keeps their squares and their score. 12c states it on the board rather than in a dialog, and the pill reads connecting in warning tone, never red.

In own-board mode, other players’ chips carry a fraction, not a board. Showing their cells would leak solutions in a mode whose whole premise is parallel play.

Annotation 20 · new · provisional

Two new components, proposed — not renamed

BoardCompare — the side-by-side finished-picture grid in 05c/13a/13b. Two up on a phone, more per row above 768px. It is not PicturePreview re-scoped: previews show one picture, this one ranks several and carries identity.

ConnectionPill — today the connection chip is inline markup in HudFrame. It has three states, a warning tone that must never be red, and an offline variant Part B also needs on Home. Worth extracting; HudFrame’s connection prop stays exactly as it is.

Both are proposals. Nothing existing is renamed or re-scoped by this pass.
